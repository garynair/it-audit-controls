# Contributing

Thank you for considering a contribution. This list covers **COBIT**, **COSO Internal Control**, **COSO Enterprise Risk Management**, and **ITGC/ITAC**: the frameworks and testing vocabulary behind most SOX 404 and financial-statement IT audit work.

## What belongs here

- Official standards, frameworks, and their supporting documents.
- Implementation guidance: control matrices (RCMs), scoping methodologies, audit programs.
- Open-source tools that automate evidence-gathering for ITGC/ITAC testing.
- Commercial GRC and SOX-management platforms with a mature, named feature set.
- Certification and training paths for IT auditors and governance practitioners.
- Government and standards bodies that own or maintain the frameworks.

## What does not belong here

- Generic accounting or financial-audit content with no IT-controls angle.
- Frameworks outside this list's current scope (NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and PCI-DSS are covered by the [Security Frameworks](https://github.com/garynair/security-frameworks) companion list; HIPAA, HITECH, and HITRUST are covered by a healthcare compliance companion list).
- Vendor marketing content without a substantive free tier, open-source component, or named feature.
- Self-promotion of products you have not used or do not work with.

## How to submit

1. Open an issue titled `[Submission]: Resource Name`, or open a PR directly.
2. Add the entry in alphabetical order within the relevant section, in the format: `- [Name](URL) - one sentence on what it is, one sentence on why a practitioner would care.`
3. No marketing language, no unverified performance claims.
4. If you are the author or maintainer of the resource, disclose that in the issue or PR description.

## Style

- American English in the description copy.
- No emojis.
- Avoid banned vocabulary (delve, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, holistic, transformative, paradigm). Plain language wins.
- Flag paywalled standards explicitly (as the COSO entries do) rather than implying free access.

## Quality bar

- Must be publicly accessible (open-source, free tier, or the standard's official paywalled source), or a mature commercial platform with a named feature set.
- Must be actively maintained (commit within 12 months, or a standards body with an active update cycle).
- Description must be factual.

## Review

A maintainer reviews PRs within seven days. Most PRs that meet the criteria above land within two weeks.
